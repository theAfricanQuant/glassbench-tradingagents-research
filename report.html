Useful research harnesses.
Wrong place for trading conviction.

GlassBench and TradingAgents make LLM-agent trading experiments visible and auditable. They are worth studying for slow, equity-oriented research. They should not replace the workspace’s price-action, FX, gold, futures, or quantified backtesting process.

Bottom line

Do not adopt either as a live strategy engine.

Keep TradingView/vectorbt-style, rule-defined research as the source of truth for tradeable hypotheses. Treat these projects as an optional laboratory for answering a narrower question: can an LLM research committee add value to a slow, liquid equity research workflow after costs and uncertainty?

The answer is not established. The tool authors themselves document non-deterministic ratings, limited pilot evidence, and paper-only broker demonstrations.

What the sources actually support

The strongest evidence argues for careful experimentation, not confidence.

  • TradingAgents repositoryIt is explicitly a research framework. The project says performance varies with model, temperature, period, and data quality; it is not investment or trading advice.
  • TradingAgents reproducibility sectionSame ticker/date runs can differ. The project attributes this to model sampling and moving live inputs. It notes that historical runs can still receive changing social/news content.
  • GlassBench backtest methodThe simulator has sensible controls. It uses weekly decisions, next-open fills, trading costs, buy-and-hold and moving-average baselines, plus a shuffled-rating placebo.
  • GlassBench pilotThe reported pilot was not encouraging. Its level-based agent trades lost money while buy-and-hold and the moving-average baseline gained. The author calls the small pilot a method check, not evidence of skill.
  • Creator video · 10:02Identical settings did not imply identical output. Two Microsoft runs, two minutes apart, produced opposite ratings. This is a critical warning against following one rating.
  • Creator video · 10:27–12:21The broker demonstration is plumbing, not validation. It is paper-only, long-only, fixed-size, and the creator says one paper order says nothing about returns.

Fit for this workspace

Where it helps, and where it conflicts with how we should trade.

Potentially useful

Slow equity research experiments

  • Audit an LLM’s equity thesis, sources, disagreement, cost, and repeatability.
  • Compare model/provider behaviour on a fixed stock-and-date grid.
  • Generate hypotheses for a separate, rule-based test—never convert prose directly into orders.
  • Study how a committee structure changes an analyst’s reasoning, rather than assuming it adds alpha.
Poor core fit

Price action, FX, gold, and futures

  • The default roles are company-fundamentals, news, social-sentiment, and indicator analysts—not bar-by-bar market-structure readers.
  • The documented simulator is weekly, long-only, and uses next-session-open fills; it cannot validate intraday execution, shorting, or futures margin behaviour.
  • FX/futures adaptation needs different data, session rules, contract rolls, costs, position sizing, and a strategy specification before LLM output is even considered.
  • An eight-minute agent run cannot serve a short-horizon price-action decision loop.
My view: the real risk is not only bad predictions. It is narrative persuasion: an elaborate committee can create a highly readable reason for a trade without producing a measurable edge. The richer the explanation, the stronger the discipline must be around out-of-sample testing and baselines.

PHASE 0

Freeze the contract

Choose one existing setup, exact period, point-in-time data snapshot, session convention, costs, and locked out-of-sample window. Record a no-trade baseline.

PHASE 1

Create a market packet

Export only causal facts: OHLC window, named levels, session state, deterministic setup flags, risk limits, and data fingerprint. No news, social feed, browser tool, or broker credential.

PHASE 2

Run shadow committees

Call a pinned TradingAgents-based workflow only on that packet. Force JSON: no_trade | long | short, invalidation, confidence, reasons, and rule citations. Repeat identical packets to measure instability.

PHASE 3

Score, then decide

Feed the structured decision into the existing simulator as a filter, never as an order source. Compare with the identical deterministic setup, no-trade, shuffled decisions, and repeated-run agreement on the locked holdout.

If we decide to explore it

A safe evaluation protocol

Do not begin with broker access, a large watchlist, or a custom fork. Start with a bounded research question and stop if the harness cannot meet it.

  1. Research-only sandbox. Use a clean local installation, no broker variables, no account connection, and one recorded engine version/commit.
  2. Choose an equity question the framework matches. Two liquid US equities, weekly decisions, and a pre-declared holding rule are sufficient for a first method test.
  3. Freeze the experimental design. Fix provider, model IDs, temperature where honoured, analyst set, debate depth, dates, transaction costs, and decision-to-position mapping before looking at returns.
  4. Measure instability. Repeat each ticker/date at least several times. Track rating agreement and the dispersion of decision levels before studying P&L.
  5. Use real baselines. Compare against buy-and-hold, a simple moving-average rule, and a shuffled-rating placebo; use next-bar/open fills and realistic costs.
  6. Separate development and final evaluation. Keep a final date range untouched until all model and rule choices are frozen. No broker consideration unless the untouched test clears pre-declared risk and robustness gates.
Adoption gate
────────────
No point-in-time leakage demonstrated     → stop

What a genuine adaptation would require

It is a separate research build, not a configuration change.

Required layerWhat must be replaced or added for price-action instruments
Data contractPoint-in-time TradingView or broker OHLCV for the exact FX pair, gold instrument, or futures contract; documented timezone, sessions, rollover, and missing-bar policy.
Research rolesMarket-structure / context / risk roles defined from explicit rules, replacing company fundamentals and social-sentiment narratives.
Decision modelA constrained setup schema: direction, entry trigger, invalidation, stop, target, time stop, risk amount, and “no trade.” Natural-language explanation is secondary.
SimulatorIntraday fills, spread/slippage, commissions, leverage, contract multipliers, shorting, session exits, and risk limits.
ValidationWalk-forward splits, parameter lock-down, repeated model runs, and comparison against a deterministic implementation of the same setup rules.
Practical conclusion: if we ever build this, the deterministic price-action specification and simulator should come first. An LLM committee would be an optional research overlay, not the component that decides whether a setup exists.
